AI Flagged 36 Articles. Only 18 Needed Changing.

September 2026 · Janeera D A · 4 min read
AI Documentation MCP

Last week I handed an AI model our PRD for a new feature, connected it to our live documentation through MCP, and asked one question: which help articles need updating?

It came back fast. 36 articles. 10 high priority, 6 medium, 20 low. My first thought: this just saved me a day of digging through the knowledge base by hand.

Then I went through them one by one.

Four of the "high priority" articles didn't need to change at all. Two of the "medium priority" ones turned out to be just as urgent as the top of the list. By the time I finished, the real number wasn't 36. It was 18.

Where it earned its keep

What surprised me was how it handled being wrong. When I pointed out a miss, it didn't dig in, it re-checked against the PRD and agreed. It also caught things I would have missed on a manual pass, like the same paragraph quietly repeated across three different articles that needed cleaning up independently.

Where the prompting mattered

One lesson cost me some rework: ask it to scan the whole feature in one go, and it recycles the same fix across every article, even the ones that don't need it. Ask article by article, with the specific section and context for each, and it gets noticeably sharper.

PRD in, MCP-connected access to the live docs, AI generates the shortlist, human re-ranks it and checks every line before anything ships.

The takeaway

That's turning into a repeatable step for us. The model replaced the blank page, the hours of manually cross-referencing a PRD against dozens of articles to find what's impacted. It didn't replace the read-through. Every one of those 18 edits still got checked line by line before publishing, and deciding what actually mattered to the reader was still a judgment call the model couldn't make on its own.

If you're using AI for documentation impact analysis, I'd be curious how you're splitting that work between the model and the review.
