What Do You Measure When AI Answers Before Anyone Opens the Page

September 2026 · Janeera D A · 3 min read
AI Documentation Analytics

Documentation has been measured the same way for decades: page views, time on page, search hits. All of that quietly assumes one thing, that a person lands on a page and reads it.

That assumption is breaking. When someone asks an AI assistant a question and gets a direct answer, they never open the article. Page views drop. The dashboard looks worse. And the documentation is often doing its job better than it ever did, the reader got what they needed without having to dig for it.

The problem isn't that documentation stopped working. It's that we're still measuring it like nothing's changed since search results were just a list of blue links.

What I think should replace page views

  • Answer accuracy. Is what the AI told the user actually correct against the source content?
  • Grounding rate. Can that answer be traced back to something real in the docs, or is the model filling in gaps on its own?
  • Intent coverage. Out of the real questions people ask, how much of that content can actually answer?
  • Unanswered-query rate and deflection. How often does a question go unanswered, and how often does existing content stop someone from needing to ask a human at all?

None of this needs new tooling

The reassuring part is that every one of these can be instrumented with data most teams already have: web analytics, search logs, and whatever the AI assistant is already logging. This isn't a case for buying a new platform, it's a different way of reading the data you're already sitting on.

Where I'm testing this

I'm applying this against a live knowledge base with an AI assistant in front of it. I don't have the full before-and-after picture yet. When I do, I'll write up what the old metrics hid and what the new ones caught instead.

Success increasingly means the reader never had to open the page. If your metrics only count the page open, you're measuring the failures and missing the wins.

Curious whether other documentation teams are already tracking anything like this, or if everyone's still reporting page views because that's what the dashboard shows by default.
